        How Pagemarks works

        Link health colors
        • Working: the page opened normally.
        • Moved: the address now leads somewhere else. Use Update link, unless it lands on a home or sign-in page (then the page is probably gone).
        • Broken: missing, server error, or refused to everyone. Or you marked it broken yourself.
        • Couldn't check: the site blocks automated checks or needs a sign-in. Usually fine. Open it, then use It works or Mark as broken.
        • May be unsafe: the website may not be the one you saved anymore. It has a new owner, is on a list of harmful sites, looks parked or for sale, or sends visitors somewhere unrelated. Pagemarks asks before opening it.
        • Working, confirmed by you: the checker can't reach the site, but you said it works. The checker keeps watching it, and it turns red or orange if the site disappears, says “not found” or moves. After 6 months Pagemarks asks you again.
        • Not checked yet: new, or its address was just changed.
        The daily link check
        • Runs once every 24 hours, usually between 2:00 and 3:30 AM Eastern Time (ET). To run it now: GitHub → Actions → Check links → Run workflow.
        • A page must fail two checks in a row before it turns red, so a short outage isn't flagged.
        • A plain “403 Forbidden” counts as broken. Bot-protection screens stay gray.
        • Why some pages show Couldn't check: the checker runs on GitHub's computers in a data center, and many sites turn away every visitor from data centers to keep out bots. The site refuses before saying whether the page exists. Your own browser isn't turned away, so you can tell.
        • For those, open the page yourself (in Organize), then click It works or Mark as broken. It works shows a green ring and counts under Working.
        • It works doesn't stop the checking. If the checker later sees clear proof that the page is gone (“not found”, or the site no longer exists) or has moved, it cancels your confirmation. Otherwise, after 6 months, Pagemarks asks you again.
        • A page that loads but says “Page not found” counts as Broken too: the weekly check reads each page's title. Sites that turn the checker away can still hide this, which is why Pagemarks asks again after 6 months.
        • Your Mark as broken always wins over the checker. Your It works only fills in where the checker couldn't tell.
        Safety checks
        • Old websites sometimes expire and are bought by someone else, who uses the old address to send visitors to ads, scams or harmful sites. Every day the checker also looks for signs of that:
        • A new owner: public registry records show when a website's address was last registered. If that's after you saved the Pagemark, the website has probably changed hands. Checked about once a week.
        • Harmful-site lists: the website, and anywhere it now sends you, is checked every day against Cloudflare's lists of harmful and adult sites (the same check used when you save).
        • Parked or for sale: pages like “This domain is for sale”. Checked about once a week.
        • Sent somewhere unrelated: a page that now bounces visitors to a different website, especially throwaway-style ones (ending in .site, .top, .xyz and similar).
        • A Pagemark that may be unsafe gets a red diamond and a May be unsafe label, even outside Organize. Clicking it asks before opening. You can Look for an archived copy of the original, Mark as broken, or choose It's fine if you're sure.
        • Looks different: once a week the checker compares each page's title now with the title you saved. If they have nothing in common (say, “Jes Liao — Paintings” has become “Best Casino Bonuses”), the Pagemark gets a Looks different note in Organize. Websites do get redesigned, so it's a gentle note rather than a warning, and It's fine clears it.
        • A real browser, weekly: once a week every Pagemark is also opened in an invisible Chrome browser on GitHub's computers, which runs the page's scripts and notes where it really ends up and what it's titled. Some harmful sites only redirect real browsers, so this catches more of them. Those computers are wiped after each run.
        • A live check when you open one: when you click a Pagemark, your browser first asks Cloudflare whether the website is on its harmful or adult list right now, since it may have been added since the last daily check. It takes a moment; if it's listed, Pagemarks asks before opening.
        • Only website names are looked up. Some harmful sites show the checker a harmless page, so no check is perfect: if a Pagemark ever takes you somewhere strange, close it and use Mark as broken.
        Organizing
        • Organize shows the tools; Done (or Esc) returns to the calm view.
        • A folder stays when you move its Pagemarks out, but when you delete Pagemarks, any folder they leave empty is removed too. Delete folder only appears when a folder is empty, and Remove empty folders (in Organize) clears out any that are left.
        • A folder shows its own Pagemarks, with sub-folders as rows on top. Sidebar counts include sub-folders.
        • Move things by dragging onto a folder, or with Move to folder…
        • Tidy up (in Organize) walks you through your Unfiled Pagemarks one at a time, with any suggested folders first.
        Home, Star and Pin
        • ☆ Star a Pagemark (in Organize) to put it on Home. Home shows your starred Pagemarks grouped by folder; click a folder's name to open it.
        • Click the ★ again to take it off Home. It stays in its folder.
        • Pin (in Organize) keeps a Pagemark at the top of its folder, above the sort order. Pinned ones show a small pin.
        • They're separate: Star is for Home, Pin is for order inside a folder. A Pagemark can be both.
        • Using both: on Home, starred Pagemarks are grouped by folder, and a pinned one goes to the top of its folder's group. The rest follow in A–Z order.
        • Pin on Home (in Organize, on a folder's page) moves that folder's group to the top of Home. On Home itself, Organize shows a Pin button on each folder card too. Pinned groups come first, in the order you pinned them; the rest follow A–Z.
        The Librarian
        • The Librarian is Pagemarks' AI helper. When you ask, you choose one of two jobs.
        • File my Unfiled Pagemarks suggests a folder for each Unfiled Pagemark (up to 2,000). The suggestions wait in Review suggestions and Tidy up.
        • Review my whole library looks over everything. It suggests folders to merge or rename, Pagemarks that seem to be in the wrong folder, and folders for your Unfiled ones. Each suggestion says why, the strongest come first, and there are never more than about 50.
        • The review leaves alone anything you've moved, and any folder you've created, renamed or moved, in the last 30 days.
        • Nothing changes until you accept it. In The Librarian's review (in Organize), accept or skip each suggestion. Undo this review puts everything back as it was, until you click Finish.
        • Suggestions are usually ready within an hour, often sooner, and you can keep using Pagemarks or close it while the Librarian works. Nothing you do will interrupt it. The Librarian looks at your library as it was when you asked, so if you move a Pagemark yourself while it works, the review might still suggest moving it. The review points these out with a note (for example, “You've moved this since the review started”), and you can click Skip to keep your own choice.
        • Each time you ask is one Librarian visit. The owner decides how many visits each person has.
        • Suggestions are made with the help of AI. Only the titles and web addresses of Unfiled Pagemarks are sent.

        Named in memory of Bill, a reference librarian and friend, whose idea of checking for broken links helped start Pagemarks.

        Searching
        • Search looks through titles, web addresses, descriptions, notes, folders and tags. Every word you type must match.
        • It always searches all your Pagemarks, whichever page you're on.
        • Clicking anywhere in the sidebar (or a folder name in a result) clears the search and takes you there. Esc or ✕ clears it too.
        • To clear it any time, click ✕ in the search box or press Esc.
        Content rules
        • Pagemarks doesn't allow adult, violent, hateful or harmful content.
        • Each Pagemark is checked when it's saved or imported: a short list of blocked sites, a word filter (titles, notes, tags, folder names, web addresses) and Cloudflare's family-safe check for adult and malware sites. Only the site name is sent to Cloudflare.
        • If something isn't allowed, it simply isn't saved. Nobody is blocked or penalized automatically.
        • Only a count of refused saves is kept for each account, never which site or text was refused. The owner can see the counts in the Invite list.

        Browser button

        The Pagemarks button saves the page you're on in one click: click it, or press Alt+M, then Save. It works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi on a computer.

        If Alt+M does nothing (a button added before it changed from Alt+P can keep the old key), set it yourself: in Chrome or Edge, open the Extensions menu and choose Keyboard shortcuts; in Firefox, type about:addons in the address bar, then click ⚙ and Manage Extension Shortcuts. Choose Alt+M for “Save this page as a Pagemark”.

        Save from your phone

        Save a page from any app's Share menu, the same way you'd send it to someone.

        Android (Chrome)
        1. Open www.pagemarks.com in Chrome and sign in.
        2. Tap ⋮ (top right), then Add to Home screen (it may say Install app), then Install.
        3. That's it. In any app, tap Share and choose Pagemarks. The Save a Pagemark window opens with the page filled in.
        iPhone and iPad

        First, to open Pagemarks like an app: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

        To add Pagemarks to the Share menu, make a small Shortcut once (about 2 minutes):

        1. Open the Shortcuts app and tap +. Name it Save to Pagemarks.
        2. Tap ⓘ (or the name at the top, then Details) and turn on Show in Share Sheet. Tap Done.
        3. At the top, set it to receive URLs and Safari web pages.
        4. Add these actions, in order:
          • Get URLs from Input (it uses the Shortcut Input)
          • URL Encode (it uses the URLs)
          • Text, and type: https://www.pagemarks.com/share/?url= then tap Variables (or the ✨ button) and choose URL Encoded Text right after the = sign
          • Open URLs (it uses the Text)
        5. Tap Done. Now in Safari or any app, tap Share, then Save to Pagemarks.

        Made it once? In the Shortcuts app, press and hold it, then Share → Copy iCloud Link, and send that link to family. They just tap Add Shortcut.

          Backups Only you see this

          Every day at about 2 AM Eastern, each person's Pagemarks and folders are copied to a private backup, kept for 14 days. To bring someone's library back:

          1. On GitHub, open the repository, then Actions → Restore Pagemarks → Run workflow.
          2. Enter their sign-in email and the backup date (YYYY-MM-DD). Leave add-missing chosen to bring back deleted Pagemarks only, or choose replace to make the library exactly as it was that day.
          3. Leave Just report on yes the first time, and read the result. Then run it again with no to do it.
