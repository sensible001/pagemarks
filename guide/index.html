Pagemarks

Getting started

A 3-minute guide

Pagemarks keeps the web pages you want to come back to: recipes, articles, lessons, anything. It checks them every day, so you'll know if one stops working.

1. Save a page

  1. Click + Add at the top.
  2. Paste the page's web address. Click Look up to fill in its title, or type your own.
  3. Choose a folder if you like (or leave it Unfiled for now), then click Save.

Even quicker: the browser button. If you use Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer, open Settings (⚙) and look for Browser button. Once it's added, click the Pagemarks button on any page, or press Alt + P, then Save.

2. Find it again

  • Home shows your favorites. Click Organize, then the ☆ star on any Pagemark to put it there.
  • Folders are listed on the left. Click one to open it.
  • Search at the top looks through all your Pagemarks (titles, addresses, notes, tags and folders), whichever page you are on. Clear it to go back where you were.
  • Read later collects pages you've set aside for later.

3. Keep it tidy

Click Organize to see the tools. Click Done to go back to the calm view.

  • Move a Pagemark by dragging it onto a folder, or tick it and use Move to folder…
  • New folder and Rename are in Organize too. Folders can have folders inside them.
  • Pin keeps a Pagemark at the top of its folder. If it's also starred, it goes to the top of that folder's group on Home too, so your most-used page is always first.
  • Pin on Home works on whole folders. Open a folder, click Organize, then Pin on Home, and that folder's group moves to the top of Home. You can also click Organize on Home and use the Pin button on any folder card.
  • Tidy up goes through your Unfiled Pagemarks one at a time, so you can file each one.
  • If you've been given visits from the Librarian, Pagemarks' AI helper, it can suggest a folder for each Unfiled Pagemark, or review your whole library and suggest folders to merge or rename and Pagemarks that seem misplaced. Nothing changes until you accept it, and a whole-library review can be undone.

4. What the colored marks mean

Every day, Pagemarks checks that your saved pages still open and are still safe. A small mark beside each one shows what it found:

  • Green dot: working.
  • Red dot: broken. The page is gone. Pagemarks may offer an archived copy.
  • Amber dot: moved. The page has a new address. In Organize, Update link fixes it.
  • Gray dot: couldn't check. Some websites turn away automatic checks. Open it yourself, then choose It works or Mark as broken.
  • Green ring: working, as you confirmed.
  • Amber ring: looks different. The page has changed completely since you saved it.
  • Red diamond: may be unsafe. The website may have a new owner or be on a list of harmful sites. Pagemarks asks before opening it.

You don't need to do anything about the colors; they're there to help when you want them. Settings explains each one in more detail.

5. Bring your favorites

  1. In Edge: press Ctrl + Shift + O, click ⋯, then Export favorites.
    In Chrome: press Ctrl + Shift + O, click ⋮, then Export bookmarks.
    In Firefox: press Ctrl + Shift + O, click Import and Backup, then Export Bookmarks to HTML (not Backup, which makes a file only Firefox can read).
  2. In Pagemarks, open Settings (⚙), find Import favorites, and choose the file you saved.

Each favorite becomes a Pagemark, and any you already have are skipped. Afterwards, Tidy up helps you file them.

6. On a phone or tablet

Pagemarks works in your phone's web browser. To open it like an app: on an iPhone, in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, in Chrome, tap ⋮, then Add to Home screen.

Save from any app. On Android, once Pagemarks is on your Home screen, it appears in every app's Share menu. On an iPhone, a small Shortcut adds it there. Settings (⚙) → Save from your phone shows how, step by step.

Questions?

Ask the person who invited you. Settings (⚙) also has short explanations of everything, under How Pagemarks works.
