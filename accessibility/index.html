Pagemarks

Accessibility

Last updated: November 24, 2026

In short: Pagemarks is meant to be easy to read and easy to use for everyone, including people who use a keyboard instead of a mouse, a screen reader, or larger text. It aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, Level AA.

What Pagemarks does

  • Readable text: a clear serif typeface at a comfortable size, with dark text on a light, low-glare background.
  • Enough contrast: text, buttons, link-health dots and stars meet the WCAG contrast levels.
  • Zoom and small screens: you can zoom to 400% or use a phone, and everything still fits without scrolling sideways.
  • Keyboard: everything can be reached with the Tab key and used with Enter or Space, with a visible outline showing where you are. A “Skip to your Pagemarks” link jumps past the menus.
  • Screen readers: buttons, form fields and icons have names; pages have headings and landmarks; messages such as “Saved” are announced.
  • Not by color alone: each link-health dot also has a name (for example “Working” or “Couldn't check”), and broken or moved pages are labeled in words.
  • Time to read: messages stay on screen until you click OK, and nothing disappears on a timer.
  • Other ways to do things: moving folders and Pagemarks by dragging is optional; the Move folder… and Move to folder… buttons do the same without a mouse.

How it was checked

In November 2026, every screen of Pagemarks (sign-in, the list, Organize, Settings, adding and editing, Tidy up, Review suggestions, the browser-button save window and this site's policy pages) was checked with an automated accessibility checker against WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA, and by hand with the keyboard. All issues found were fixed.

Known limits

  • Pagemarks hasn't yet been tested by people who use screen readers every day. Automated checks can't catch everything.
  • Signing in uses Google's own sign-in window, and the pages you save belong to other websites. Their accessibility is up to them.
  • The optional browser button is a single click or the keyboard shortcut Alt+P; its small save window follows the same standards as Pagemarks.

Tell us about a problem

If something in Pagemarks is hard to see, read or use, please tell the person who invited you. It will be looked at and, where possible, fixed.
